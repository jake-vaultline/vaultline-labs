What it tells you

Everything by format

Video, photo, audio and project files — counts, sizes, and which codecs and resolutions are eating the space.

Which cameras shot it

Read from clip metadata, sidecars and card structure. Usually the line people screenshot.

What's biggest

Largest folders and largest files, with true recursive sizes rather than whatever Finder felt like reporting.

How far back it goes

Date range and storage by year, from capture dates rather than file timestamps.

What's wasting space

Likely duplicate files and how much you'd get back, empty folders left by aborted offloads, and an estimate of what proxies would cost.

A report you can send

Export the whole thing as PDF, HTML or CSV and hand it to whoever asked how full the drive was.

About the privacy claim

It cannot reach the network. Verify it yourself.

Most apps promise not to upload your data. This one is sandboxed by macOS and never requested network permission at all, so the promise is enforced by the operating system rather than by us. Run this on the downloaded app:

codesign -d --entitlements - /Applications/VaultlineLabsDriveInspector.app

You'll see the sandbox and read-only file access. You won't see a network entitlement, because there isn't one. The app can only ever read the drive or folder you hand it — nothing else on your Mac.

Installing

  1. Download and open the DMG, then drag the app to Applications.
  2. Launch it. It's notarized by Apple, so it opens normally — no right-click trick, no security warning to click through.
  3. Drag a drive onto the window, or use Choose Folder. That's the permission — there's no Full Disk Access to grant and no trip to System Settings.
